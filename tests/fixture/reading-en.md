# Reading modes

Most documents are written for people who will read them once and then act on them. A good reader moves fast through the parts that say what they expected and slows down where something new appears. Reading modes try to help with that, without changing a single word of the page.

The cat sat on the mat while the extraordinary news about the budget spread through the office. Nobody was surprised by the meeting itself, because the same meeting happens every week, but the figure of $4.2M in the third paragraph made several people stop and read it twice.

> A quote can carry a long thought across several lines, so that a reader has a place to rest between the steps of an argument.

Here is a list that a page might contain:

- the first item explains why the work started in January
- the second item describes what changed after the first review
- the third item lists what is still waiting on a decision

A table is not prose, and the first cell of each row stays bright:

| Name | Role |
|---|---|
| Plot | the company that raised the money |
| Ethan | the person who wrote the first draft |

Code is never touched by a reading mode: `const total = items.length;` and the block below keep their own colours.

```js
function sum(a, b) {
  return a + b;
}
```

Links keep their own colour too, as in [the project page](https://example.com/page), and **bold text stays bold** because it is already an emphasis the writer chose. The last paragraph is long enough to wrap onto several lines in a normal browser window, which matters for any mode that follows the line breaks, because the reader needs a stable idea of where each line begins and where it ends before the eye jumps to the next one.
